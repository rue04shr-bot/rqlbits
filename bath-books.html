books i've read in the bathroom — a definitive tier list

waterproofing optional. emotional damage guaranteed.

📖

first of all, let me clarify something before the western girlies with bathtubs arrive.

when i say “bath reading”, i do NOT mean those pinterest aesthetic bubble baths with candles and wine glasses and suspiciously expensive white bathrooms.

i mean indian bathroom culture.

i mean sitting on the closed toilet seat at 1am because everyone else is asleep and it's the only room where nobody asks: “beta abhi tak jag rahi ho?”

“ARE YOU ALIVE?? ITNI DER SE ANDAR KYA KAR RAHI HO??”

truly no greater oppression exists.

anyway here is my completely unnecessary ranking of books i've emotionally attached to while hiding from my family in the bathroom.

S-TIER: changed my brain chemistry permanently

Animal Farm — George Orwell

nothing prepares you for reading about corrupt pigs while your mother is outside the door asking if you've fallen unconscious.

this book genuinely made me stare at the bucket for ten straight minutes processing capitalism.

also every character somehow reminded me of indian politicians which was deeply concerning.

Palace of Illusions — Chitra Banerjee Divakaruni

this book had me feeling emotions i didn't even know were installed in my system.

draupadi was carrying feminism, rage, heartbreak, intelligence, beauty, and emotional damage ALL AT ONCE. genuinely iconic behaviour.

i read one chapter and suddenly started staring dramatically into the mirror like i was in a sanjay leela bhansali film.

also impossible to explain to family because how do you say: “sorry i was crying over mahabharata retelling in the bathroom.”

A-TIER: made me temporarily unbearable

Can't Hurt Me — David Goggins

reading this while sitting comfortably in bathroom slippers felt extremely disrespectful to david goggins personally.

every chapter sounded like: “your limits are fake. go run 80km immediately.”

sir respectfully i got tired climbing stairs.

still, this book somehow convinced me at 2am that i was about to transform my entire life.

did i wake up productive next morning? absolutely not.

The Secret Diary of Adrian Mole

this book feels exactly like reading someone's private twitter drafts from 2009.

adrian mole is simultaneously: deeply annoying, weirdly relatable, emotionally exhausting, and somehow hilarious.

peak bathroom reading honestly because every five minutes i had to pause and whisper: “bro WHAT are you doing.”

B-TIER: self-help books trying to fix my attention span

How to Finish Everything You Start

the irony of procrastinating this book for four months before finally reading it in one sitting inside the bathroom is honestly cinematic.

it did teach me discipline though. for approximately: 17 minutes.

still better than every productivity reel made by a 19-year-old millionaire with a podcast mic.

final conclusion: bathroom reading in indian households is less of a hobby and more of a survival strategy.

because the second you sit peacefully with a book, your family starts behaving like you've disappeared during a national emergency.

anyway if anyone needs me i'll be hiding in the bathroom pretending to shampoo my hair while rereading emotionally devastating literature.
